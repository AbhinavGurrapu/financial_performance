# Step 14C — Comparison of Mechanical What-If Scenarios

## Executive Summary

This report provides a comparative synthesis of the three mechanical what-if scenarios evaluated for the **CY2012 USD** Reseller Sales scope (`CurrencyKey = 100`, `CalendarYear = 2012`):

1. **Scenario 1 (Product Mix Rebalancing)**: Shifting 5% of CY2012 unit volume from Road Bikes and Touring Bikes to Mountain Bikes.
2. **Scenario 2 (Targeted Price Realization)**: Applying a 2% realized price increase to Road Bikes and Touring Bikes.
3. **Scenario 3 (Targeted Discount Reduction)**: Reducing line-item discount rates by 50% for Accessories and Clothing.

## 2. Analytical Responses & Synthesis

### A. Largest Mechanical Gross Profit Impact
**Scenario 2 (Targeted Price Realization)** produced the largest mechanical Gross Profit impact, generating **+$203,994.37** in incremental Gross Profit (**+30.23% improvement** over baseline), bringing portfolio Gross Profit from $674,861.75 to **$878,856.11** (increasing Gross Margin from 3.23% to 4.16%).

### B. Smallest Mechanical Gross Profit Impact
**Scenario 3 (Targeted Discount Reduction)** produced the smallest mechanical impact, generating **+$3,291.42** in incremental Gross Profit (**+0.49% improvement** over baseline), bringing portfolio Gross Profit to **$678,153.17** (Gross Margin from 3.23% to 3.24%).

### C. Relative Financial Scenario Impact Analysis

#### Price Realization (Largest Tested Scenario Impact)
- **Price Realization (Scenario 2)** produced the largest impact **UNDER THE TESTED SCENARIO ASSUMPTIONS** (+2% price realization increase on Road & Touring Bikes yielded +$203.99k GP).
- **Product Mix (Scenario 1)** produced the second-largest impact under its specific 5% volume-shift assumption (+5% unit shift from Road & Touring to Mountain Bikes yielded +$93.55k GP).
- **Discount Reduction (Scenario 3)** produced the smallest impact under its specific 50% discount-reduction assumption (50% cut in Accessories & Clothing discount rates yielded +$3.29k GP).
- **Important Methodological Note**: The three scenarios use different intervention sizes (2% price adjustment vs. 5% unit volume shift vs. 50% discount rate reduction), so their dollar impacts should **NOT** be interpreted as a controlled comparison of intrinsic lever effectiveness.

### D. Lever to Investigate First
Based on the tested mechanical scenarios, management should investigate targeted price realization first because it produced the largest modeled Gross Profit opportunity (+$203.99k) under the tested assumptions, subject to validating commercial feasibility and potential volume response.

### E. Secondary & Tertiary Levers
- **Secondary Lever**: **Product Mix Rebalancing (Scenario 1)** should be investigated second. Shifting volume away from value-destructive bike lines eliminates structural losses and provides substantial profit upside (+$93.55k under the 5% volume shift assumption).
- **Tertiary Lever**: **Discount Reduction (Scenario 3)** should be treated as secondary/tertiary for these specific categories because baseline discount leakage in Accessories and Clothing was already minimal ($6.58k total), offering modest profit recovery potential ($3.29k under the 50% discount reduction assumption).

### F. Scenarios Where Tested Actions Do NOT Fully Solve Profitability
**None of the three scenarios individually solve the underlying profitability problem for Touring Bikes**:
- In **Scenario 2**, increasing Touring Bikes realized ASP by 2% (from $687.21 to $700.95) leaves its unit Gross Profit severely negative at **-$232.83 / unit** (versus -$246.57 baseline), because standard cost remains far higher at **$933.78 / unit**. A 2% price increase reduces the loss per unit by only $13.74, leaving Touring Bikes in a deep structural gross loss (-$128.52k total loss).
- In **Scenario 1**, shifting 5% of Touring Bikes volume away removes only 27.60 units, leaving 524.4 units generating **-$129.30k in ongoing gross losses**.
- **Conclusion**: Incremental price realization (2%) or small volume shifts (5%) are insufficient to overcome severe unit-level negative cost-price spread in Touring Bikes without structural cost reduction or larger price repositioning.

## 3. Methodological & Analytical Distinctions

To ensure objective financial communication, four distinct analytical dimensions are explicitly preserved:

1. **Observed Historical Findings (Empirical Data)**:
   - Reseller Sales CY2012 Net Revenue was **$20,908,342.06**, Gross Profit was **$674,861.75** (3.23% Gross Margin).
   - Road Bikes (-$351,997.27 GP) and Touring Bikes (-$136,107.21 GP) were severely value-destructive in CY2012.
   - Accessories ($1,545.93 discount) and Clothing ($5,036.91 discount) had low baseline discount rates (1.55% and 0.95%).

2. **Mechanical Scenario Results (Mathematical Sensitivity Output)**:
   - Scenario 1 (Mix): +$227.01k Net Revenue, +$93.55k Gross Profit (+13.86% GP improvement).
   - Scenario 2 (Price): +$203.99k Net Revenue, +$203.99k Gross Profit (+30.23% GP improvement).
   - Scenario 3 (Discount): +$3.29k Net Revenue, +$3.29k Gross Profit (+0.49% GP improvement).

3. **Assumptions (Held Constant for Accounting Models)**:
   - Zero price elasticity of demand (no volume loss when price increases by 2%).
   - Constant demand & production capacity (Mountain Bikes can sell +722.4 units at current weighted ASP/cost).
   - Fixed cost structure and discount behavior.

4. **Recommendations (Framed Decision Directives)**:
   - **Recommended Framing**: *"Based on the tested mechanical scenarios, management should investigate targeted price realization first because it produced the largest modeled Gross Profit opportunity (+$203.99k) under the tested assumptions, subject to validating commercial feasibility and potential volume response, while simultaneously evaluating structural cost/repositioning options for Touring Bikes where a 2% price increase leaves unit margin deeply negative (-$232.83/unit)."*
